# The Whole Bill — working notes

A calculator that shows a household what its healthcare actually costs — including the employer
premium share that never appears on a paystub — what the alternatives cost, and what universal
coverage would cost instead. It then drafts a letter to their representative.

    ./build.sh && node test/test.mjs      # or: npm test

`src/page.html` is the source of truth. `dist/` is generated — never edit it.
`src/calc.js` has no dependencies and runs in node or a browser; the benchmark table
(`src/benchmarks.js`, generated by `tools/build-benchmarks.mjs`) is injected into it via
`W.useBenchmarks()` — the page does this on load, node callers must do it themselves.

**Lessons log.** This project's entries (WB-nnn) are in `docs/LESSONS.md`; add one the same
day something here costs money, time, a broken deploy or a wrong number in front of readers.
The owner's cross-project rules (G-nnn) live in the private `tylerzeruk-glitch/Personal` repo
(`lessons/global.md`); read them before starting new work when you have access

## The one rule everything else serves

**This site's only asset is being trustworthy about numbers.** It argues a contested political
position, so the arithmetic has to survive a hostile reader. Every convention below exists
because a shortcut got caught.

## Every figure carries a basis

`calc.js` tags each returned figure:

| tag | meaning |
|---|---|
| `statutory` | fixed in law or a published federal table — exact |
| `survey` | a real published measurement, but an average (KFF) |
| `modeled` | our estimate — **must be labelled wherever it is shown** |

Never ship an unlabelled modeled figure. The UI surfaces the tag; the methodology page
(`/methodology`, its own static document; the artifact keeps `#/methodology`) shows the
arithmetic. If you add a figure, add its basis.

## Disclose provenance, including for sources you agree with

Mercatus is labelled Koch-funded. The Yale preprint is labelled as not peer reviewed, with its
author's advisory role to Sen. Sanders stated. Apply the same standard to friendly and hostile
sources or the labelling means nothing.

Where a study published no household-level figure, say so and show the allocation — see the
Blahous derivation in `calc.js`. Do not reverse-engineer a rate to land on a number you like;
an earlier version did exactly that and it was indefensible.

## Verify rendered geometry, not source

Three separate defects shipped past code review and were caught only by measuring actual pixels:

- bars silently squashed because flex children shrink by default (`flex-shrink: 0` on anything
  whose pixel size *is* the data)
- a chart that drew the same height for every route, because each route scaled to its own maximum
- chart labels colliding at incomes past the subsidy cliff

`test/test.mjs` asserts rendered geometry for exactly this reason. **Do not claim a visual
behaviour without checking the screen.** It caught the axis caption rendering at 8.5px on its
first run.

## The story and its folds (since 2026-11-12)

The page is one pinned bar beside four steps (paystub, the hidden part, the whole bill, universal
coverage), inside `.inkzone`. `storyPlan()` in the page script holds each route's steps and copy;
`renderStory()` sizes the segments on every paint and `paintStory()` decides which stand for the
step in focus. Segments are the route's own parts (`W.routeBreakdown`) and the model's
(`S.universal.parts`) on one scale shared by the route on the bar and all six models
(`storyScale()`), so a segment's height is always value / scale. It is not shared across
routes (owner, 2026-11-12): borrowing the marketplace's larger total left an empty sliver
above an employer household's whole bill. The comparison fold, which shows the routes
together, keeps its own shared scale. `--H` (the bar's height in px) and `--sticky-h` are set
by `sizeStory()`; the stylesheet never guesses them.

Every chart the long page used to show at once (calculator inputs, waterfall, rise, cliff or bad
year, side-by-side comparison) is a real `<details class="fold">` under the step it supports,
and the page has two views of them (`setView()`). Readers get only the first: the owner
withdrew the on-screen switch on 2026-11-12, and an old stored choice is cleared on load.

- **Step by step** (default, `html.sheets`): a `.wide` fold opens as a floating box over a
  dimmed page (`openSheet(d, from)`), a labelled dialog with Escape and a close button. Nothing
  underneath moves. The first build opened wide folds in the flow; the owner found that unnatural
  (a screen of chart pushed in under the step, and the bar left to make room). Since 2026-11-13
  the box grows out of whatever was tapped (the row's corner-arrows icon, the household pill's
  button, the results bar's pencil) and shrinks back into it, and **each box fits one screen**
  at 1280x800, 1440x900, 1024x768 and 390x844 (the suite checks, and checks the household box in
  every route and at the cliff, floor and Medicaid incomes; a 360x740 phone may scroll three boxes
  a little). The fit rules are scoped to `.fold.wide[open]` in the sheets view, so the one-page
  view keeps its full-size charts; on a phone the household box drops its four breakdown cards
  and the TRICARE pointer (objection 05 carries it), and compare drops the model strip. In the
  household box the inputs sit on their own panel with filled fields and the estimate reads off
  the ground beside it: inputs on the left, results on the right (owner, 2026-11-13). New copy in
  the eligibility box (`#elig`) has to fit that box; the subtitle under the total already says
  "full price", so the warnings do not repeat it.
  Folds that hold only a paragraph open in place, with the plus.
- **All on one page** (`html.onepage`): every fold stands open in the flow at full width, the
  summaries become headings, and the bar steps aside (`.stage.yield`) where a wide chart needs
  its space. Printing borrows this view, and the suite reaches it through the hidden switch.

With no script the same `<details>` simply open in place.

**On a phone (760px and under) the steps are cards** (`html.cards`, set by `applyCards()` in the
step-by-step view): `.ssteps` becomes a native scroll-snap row, the bar a slim column beside it
without labels, with ticks above and Back and Next below, and a fifth closing card. The step in
focus comes from the row's own scroll position, so swiping is the browser's scrolling and nothing
is hijacked. The first build pinned the bar across the top third of a phone; the owner's verdict
was "the bars cover half the screen". All on one page, on a phone, puts the bar away entirely.

- **The suite reads the page all on one page** (`page()` in `test/test.mjs` clicks the hidden
  switch), because most checks measure what is inside the folds. Checks about the story,
  the sheets and the hero pass `shut = true` to get the page as a reader first meets it.
- **New copy in a step goes through `storyPlan()`**, per route, and a modeled part says so in its
  bar label ("(estimate)") and in the step's basis line.
- **The teal turn re-points the grey tokens** (`.inkzone.uni`). Panels go darker than the ground
  there, not lighter: small teal and amber labels sit on them. The suite measures contrast in
  that state.
- A static `data-fig` needs a default in `tools/default-figures.mjs` or the build refuses.
- **Small text is the thing the owner keeps cutting.** A step carries one basis chip (`tag` in
  `storyPlan()`: "Survey average", "Set in law", "Includes our estimate"), not a sentence with
  links. Detail and links belong inside the fold. Before adding a note, ask whether a chip, a
  glyph or the fold can carry it.
- **Fold glyphs** (`renderGlyphs()`) are 56 by 36 drawings made from the page's own figures:
  the household as dots, the cliff as this household's curve, the arithmetic as the waterfall's
  columns. Icons, so aria-hidden, but never an invented shape; the suite checks the ones that
  carry proportion.
- **The opening is the story's first rows** (`.shero`), on the dark ground with the empty bar
  beside it. While it has the screen the zone is `.intro`: the bar's empty box, the running
  total and the results bar all wait out of sight, and a bouncing arrow (`#cue`) is the way down. There is no ZIP field on it. The household pill
  (`#hhPill`), and the household line in the results bar, open the household sheet, which
  holds the ZIP and the family details together.
- **"What changed" is one slow line of news** in a strip under the header's rule, beside the
  plan year (`.newsbar`, `details.flyout#changed`), that pops the dated list out over the page. The owner asked for the moving line on 2026-11-12,
  reversing the earlier "static, no ticker" choice. It never moves under reduced motion and
  stops under the pointer; the scrolling text is an aria-hidden copy of the entries.
- **After the turn the page stays teal**: the other number, the objections and the letter are
  `section.paper.onteal` inside `.inkzone`, with the paper tokens re-pointed. The letter is the
  exception, a sheet of paper with its own tokens put back. No section wears a number.
- **Step by step, the results bar shows only the household and the unit switch.** Its three
  totals are what the steps reveal; they return in the all-on-one-page view.
- **The six estimates are circles on a line** (`renderModelLine()`), each at the calculator's
  own total for that model and this household, with name chips under it. The chips (and the
  phone's picker) run cheapest to priciest for this household, so their order moves with
  income; address a pill by `data-m`, never by index. The dots are taps too (a 44px band; a tap
  picks the nearest dot within 24px); the chips stay the keyboard and screen-reader control, so the
  line stays aria-hidden.

## Traps that have bitten this codebase

- **`.steps` and `.step` are the household stepper.** A new container named `.steps` inherited
  its flex row and laid four story steps out side by side. Grep a class name before reusing it.
- **A closed `<details>` still reports sizes.** Chrome hides its content with content-visibility,
  so `offsetHeight` on a chart inside a shut fold is its full height. Ask `checkVisibility()`, or
  select on `[open]`.
- **A share-of-itself IntersectionObserver threshold can be unreachable.** The reveal waited for
  18% of a section to be on screen; the story is several screens tall, so its bars would have
  stayed clipped. Tall sections reveal on any intersection.
- **A shut `<details>` is a containing block for fixed children.** Its hidden content box has
  containment, so a sheet styled `position:fixed` while shut was laid out against its own card.
  Sheet rules are scoped to `[open]`.
- **Absolute children escape a scroll row.** The visually hidden fold lines (`.vh`) sat in the
  phone card row with no positioned ancestor inside it, so they were placed against the page
  and widened it to 841px at 390. `.sstep` is `position:relative` for that reason.
- **A transition in force when an element turns `position:fixed` animates from its old place.**
  The sheets slid in from the right (their spot in the flow under `left:50%`) before rising.
  Park a sheet with transitions off, then release it; the suite samples its path with motion on.
- **An entrance animation makes a stacking context.** The news strip rises in with a transform,
  so its pop-out's z-index counted only inside the strip and the headline painted over the open
  list. The suite reads with reduced motion and missed it; `.top` now sits at z-index 40, and a
  check with motion on samples the open list for anything covering it.
- **A transition can be caught by print.** The ink-to-teal background change printed mid-colour
  until `@media print` turned transitions off.

- **`overflow-x: hidden` on html/body kills `position: sticky`** — it computes `overflow-y` to
  `auto`, making them scroll containers. Use `overflow-x: clip`.
- **SVG text scales with the viewBox.** A 920-wide viewBox in a 340px column renders 11px text at
  4px. The cliff chart picks its geometry from the container width. Check the *effective* size.
- **One media query block per breakpoint.** Two `max-width: 760px` blocks meant source order
  silently decided which rules won, and the newer ones looked like they "weren't applying".
- **Chart geometry carries magnitude; colour carries category.** Never make colour do both — an
  early waterfall encoded sequence as decreasing lightness and ran out of contrast before it ran
  out of steps.
- **An inline `display` defeats the `hidden` attribute.** Inline styles beat every stylesheet
  rule including the UA's `[hidden]{display:none}` — slot 03's two route views both rendered at
  once until a geometry test caught the duplicate eyebrow. If an element toggles `hidden`, its
  `display` must live in the stylesheet, never inline.

## Money

Every figure is stored as an **annual** number and rendered through one formatter. The Year/Month
switch divides at render time. Nothing holds a pre-divided monthly value — that is what keeps the
two views from drifting and the rounding honest (the monthly waterfall bands still sum exactly to
the monthly total).

Two exceptions, both deliberate: the decade total never divides, and COBRA is authored annually
and displayed monthly.

## Language

The household does not *spend* the employer's premium share — that is the entire argument. Copy
says what healthcare **costs** a household, and reserves "spends" for money that leaves its hands.

**No em or en dashes anywhere a reader can see.** Owner's rule: readers take them as a tell of
AI-written text, and this site cannot afford the suspicion. Rewrite instead of substituting:
a comma, colon, period or parentheses for prose; "to" for ranges; "·" for label pairs (select
options, the page title, name-role pairs); a plain hyphen for empty-state placeholders and
citation page ranges. Code comments are exempt. The suite enforces this by scanning rendered
text in every copy-bearing state — new dynamic copy paths should be added to that test's loop.

## Volatile constants

Bill numbers change every Congress: one `BILL` constant in the app script (currently H.R. 3069 /
S. 1506, 119th). FPL tables, the applicable-percentage schedule, employer premiums and the
financing models all live in `calc.js` with their sources in comments.

**The enhanced premium tax credits expired 31 Dec 2025 and the 400% FPL cliff is back.** Any
figure or copy written before that assumed the old schedule and is wrong.

## Type

Two families: Newsreader for headlines, reading and figures (its own lining, tabular
figures through `--fig`; the large ones at regular weight), with its real italic for emphasis
(headlines are regular weight, sentence case, set by size); Inter for labels and controls,
roman only. On 2026-11-12 Inter replaced Hanken Grotesk for figures (the owner found Hanken's
numerals did not read well as numbers), and the same day, after six faces side by side, the
owner chose Newsreader for figures. Its digits were measured tabular with a
narrow comma before it went in; measure any replacement the same way. **Italic only in
the serif**: the sans loads no italic, so `i`/`em` inside sans notes and table cells are set
roman, and the suite fails on a third family or on any italic in the sans. Buttons do not
inherit the page font: give every button a font-family (the model pills rendered in Arial
until the font test caught it). Phone widths are checked for odd wrapping, not only overflow:
`body` inherits `text-wrap:pretty` so labels and notes do not end on one word. Details:
`docs/design-brief.md`.

## Corners

One system since 2026-11-13 (owner: "some boxes have squared edges, and some have rounded edges"):
`--r` (10px) for every box, control, card and panel; `--r-s` (4px) for small marks (checkboxes,
tags, the text highlight); `--r-lg` (16px) for a floating sheet; a pill (999px) or a circle for
round controls. Bars, bands and ranges are data and stay square. A sliding highlight takes its
button's corner. The suite fails on any box outside the system.

## Accessibility floors

4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for graphics and control boundaries, 44px touch targets. The palette table is
in `docs/design-brief.md`. The ink ground runs out of contrast quickly — do not encode meaning in
lightness there.
